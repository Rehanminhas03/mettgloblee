How to run an audit that produces decisions, not a 70-page PDF

An audit is useful when a team can understand what matters, why it matters and what happens next. Issue volume is not the same as diagnostic quality.

METTGLOBAL FIELD GUIDE · AUDITS & DIAGNOSTICS · 8 MIN READ
EVIDENCEIMPACTPRIORITYOWNERMEASURE

Separate observation from conclusion

Document what was actually observed: a broken path, slow response, stock variance, missing control, confusing page or technical error. Then explain the likely business consequence.

Score impact, not drama

Prioritize findings by severity, affected users or processes, commercial impact, confidence and remediation effort. A visually obvious issue is not automatically the most important issue.

Group root causes

Ten symptoms may come from one broken process. Group findings by underlying cause so the team fixes systems rather than repeatedly patching symptoms.

Assign an owner and next action

Every high-priority recommendation should identify who needs to act, what the first action is and what dependency could block it.

Define the evidence of improvement

Choose the measure that should change after remediation: conversion, errors, lead response time, inventory accuracy, page performance or another operating signal.

The best audit is not the one with the most findings. It is the one that changes what the team does next.
